# True

On Saturday, twelve people rang the bells of York Minster for eight hours and twenty-two minutes.

The method was Bristol Surprise Maximus. Twelve bells, each striking once per row, every row a different order. A bell may move one place per row, no more. Nobody reads anything. Each ringer carries the path of one bell in their head and watches eleven other ropes to know where they are. If a row comes round twice, the peal is false. If the band loses its place and can't recover, the peal is lost, and every hour before it counts for nothing.

Bells this size don't stop on a whim. Even the smallest is heavier than the person on its rope. The tenor at York weighs fifty-nine hundredweight, one quarter and twenty-three pounds. About three tonnes. It sounds B-flat. Ewan Hull rang it. The photograph on the record is credited to Ewan's Dad.

The record itself is short. Association, date, place, length, method, composer, tenor, twelve names, a C beside the conductor. There is a field for footnotes, where ringers write *first peal*, or *in memory of*, or *birthday compliments*. It's empty.

They rang 10,032 rows. None came round twice.
